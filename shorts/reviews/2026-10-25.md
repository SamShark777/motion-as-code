What the data says (descriptive, Oct 14–23, n = 37):
- **Retention drives Instagram reach.** r = 0.77 between IG retention and IG views. Length works against retention, weakly (r = −0.24): Shorts under 36 s had median YT 251 / IG 245 views; 36 s and longer had YT 113 / IG 188.
- **Concrete first lines win, abstract questions lose.** The top Instagram openers name a specific number, group or event ("Eight people faked ONE symptom…", "93% rated their driving above average", "two honest people remember differently"). The bottom ones are abstract "Why might… / Does… / What will you do when…" questions ("Why might the next swipe feel tempting?", "Does feeling rejected prove…", "Why can a scary headline feel…"). 35 of 37 hooks were questions, so a question-vs-claim comparison still isn't possible.
- **"Why…" titles do worse.** The 20 titles starting with "Why" had median YT 132 / IG 155 / TT 33. The 17 others had YT 252 / IG 286 / TT 42.
- **Slot:** 17:00 and 20:00 (old schedule) did best on YouTube. In the current 09/14/19 schedule (Oct 19–24, 6 Shorts per slot), 19:00 leads Instagram: median 835 views and 34% retention, vs. 14:00 at 362 / 27% and 09:00 at 189 / 29%. The samples are small.
- **TikTok is stuck at 10–120 views**, whatever the topic. Retention there is low (median 14%): viewers swipe in the first 4–5 s.

## 3 hook/topic rules for next week (for new scripts; hypotheses, not proven)

1. **Line 1 states a concrete result: a number, a group of people or an event.** Example: "Eight people faked one symptom and every hospital admitted them." Not "Why might X feel Y?". No abstract "Why might / Can X influence / What happens when" openers.
2. **Keep it at 25–32 s.** Retention is what Instagram rewards, and longer Shorts hold a smaller share. Character stories have to fit too (zoe was 54 s).
3. **At most one "Why…" title per day.** Use number-led, named-group ("Psychology of people who…") or bold-claim titles ("The Experiment That Fooled Psychiatrists").

These shape new scripts only. Per the pattern-test rules, the queue order and existing scripts were not changed.
